1 · How I'd evaluate the agents' performance on each ticket, and why

My answer (verbatim)
ok so how id evaluate performance is how many runs model calls and tokens it takes, the boss should correctly send everything to the right place the first time and then they should be able to delegate across each other efficiently with all of them aware of the bosses plan with awarness of what the others do and not have to constantly send it back to the boss to decide the next step and instead of burning tokens when it gets stuck it should just pause and ask the human for the next call if it doesnt immediately know what to do next or encounters an issue since the human is still hired to do this too by approving everything.
Evidence from this run
  • Ticket 101: resolved in 2 runs, 17 model calls, 34.4k tokens. The team spotted that open invoice 501 was the rush reprint and paid it instead of double-ordering; the human approved $840. Draft #1 gave a careful ETA (9/5, pending confirmation) and other sizes.
  • Ticket 102: resolved in 3 runs, 20 calls, 41.0k tokens, the most expensive ticket. Verification was right on the first pass (landlord, $2,400, due 9/2), but follow-up 1 couldn't confirm the payment because get_finances had no payments ledger (a tool gap, since fixed). Follow-up 2 resolved it.
  • Ticket 103: resolved in 1 run, 11 calls, 23.4k tokens. It priced correctly ($46.40, 52.6% margin) and obeyed the vendor-block rule (PO refused), with no partial shipment. Its note cites invoice 501 as the blocker, which was true at run time; the invoice was paid later.
  • Board-wide: 6 team runs, 48 model calls, 98,721 tokens, 0 rule violations, checking never negative (Cash tab).

3 · What would've been simpler as one agent with tools, and why

My answer (verbatim)
What wouldve been simpler with one agent is that all these handoffs take extra time and money by resending prompts and tool definitions and theres chekcing. its good that specialists know what to do and that they caught errors like a duplicate reprint but optimizing this I would try to utilize some kind of token caching and memory to same time and tokens long term.
Evidence from this run
  • Ticket 102 needed only 3 tools (get_lease_status, request_payment_approval, resolve_ticket) but used 3 agents and a chain of 2 handoffs. Every handoff resends prompts and tool definitions; this ticket cost 41.0k tokens.
  • Follow-ups mostly needed one check plus resolve_ticket: ticket 101's follow-up was 5 calls, and 102's last follow-up was 5 calls with a single get_finances.
  • Where the team earned its keep: separation of duties (only the accountant can queue money, only a human approves) and specialist prompts. The two most important saves were catching the duplicate reprint on 101 and the vendor block on 103.

Three new problems could be customer_order, rent_notice, or price_override tickets, for example: "a student group orders 30 Crest Mugs, which have 0 in stock, so check_stock finds the shortfall and a vendor with no unpaid invoice (Elm City Gifts), and request_purchase_order queues the restock for human approval," "next month's rent notice for 10/2 arrives, so get_lease_status verifies it and the server refuses to queue it until 10 days before it's due, just like ticket 102," and "a club asks for 50% off caps, so quote_margin shows the margin and the 50%-margin rule turns it down, like the $46.40 cap on ticket 103."
Evidence from this run

What the current tools already cover: stock and shortfall by size (check_stock), vendor ETAs and blocks, margins (quote_margin), rent verification (get_lease_status), queuing payments and prepaid POs for human approval, customer drafts, and closing tickets. Any new ticket of type customer_order, rent_notice, or price_override fits these.

5 · Three new problems this team couldn't solve with these tools, and the tools needed

My answer (verbatim)
new problems the team cant solve would be recording a sale I think the accountant would need a record_revenue function and inventory needs to recieve_stock and also inventory and accountant would need to process_refund and return_stock for refunds/returns to the store for example: "without receive_stock, ticket 101's size S reprint due 9/5 can never arrive in inventory, so the tee can never ship," and "without record_revenue, checking can only go down ($3,400 to $160 on the Cash tab), which left ticket 103's $264 restock unaffordable even after invoice 501 was paid."
Evidence from this run

What's missing today, as the three tickets showed:

  • No way to receive stock or record a sale: the 9/5 reprint for 101 can never arrive, and nothing records revenue.
  • No cash coming in: checking can only go down ($3,400 → $160), so 103's $264 restock stayed unaffordable.
  • No refunds or returns, no new vendors or quotes, and no outbound channel: drafts never leave the board, by design.
